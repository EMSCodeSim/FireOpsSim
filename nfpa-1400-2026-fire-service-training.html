NFPA 1400 (2026): What Firefighters and Training Officers Need to Know

Seven training standards now sit under one standard. Here's what to review before updating your department's training program.

The 2026 edition of NFPA 1400, Standard on Fire Service Training, consolidates training requirements previously spread across seven NFPA documents. For training officers, the practical task is not simply replacing document numbers: it is checking whether lesson plans, safety procedures, instructor responsibilities, and evaluation records still match the applicable standard.

Which standards does NFPA 1400 consolidate?

  • NFPA 1402: Fire training facilities and associated props
  • NFPA 1403: Live-fire training evolutions
  • NFPA 1404: Respiratory protection training
  • NFPA 1407: Rapid intervention crew training
  • NFPA 1408: Thermal imager training
  • NFPA 1410: Emergency scene operations training
  • NFPA 1451: Fire service vehicle operations training

What changed in 2026?

NFPA highlights updated guidance for water supplies during live-fire evolutions, training structure classifications and roof loading, alignment with NFPA 1700, revised search-and-rescue training criteria, and new material addressing technical rescue and hazardous materials response training. Review the full text rather than assuming the consolidated provisions are identical to older editions.

Training officer review checklist

  1. Inventory lesson plans, policies, and checklists citing the older standards.
  2. Prioritize high-risk evolutions: live fire, rapid intervention, respiratory protection, and vehicle operations.
  3. Check training facilities, props, water supplies, and instructor responsibilities against applicable requirements.
  4. Separate attendance from demonstrated competency; identify what was performed and who evaluated it.
  5. Confirm which edition your authority having jurisdiction and state have adopted before changing department policy.

Practice exercise: radio communication during a missing-firefighter event

Assign an incident commander, first-arriving engine, rapid intervention crew, and later-arriving units. Require crews to communicate conditions, request resources, maintain accountability, and participate in an instructor-led debrief. A tabletop or radio scenario supports practice but does not by itself establish NFPA 1400 compliance or field competency.
