Start here

Your Instructor I prep plan

Do not start by memorizing random definitions. Build the role first, then use retrieval practice and a timed exam.

Step 1

Learn the role

Work through all ten modules. Focus on what an Instructor I may do, what requires a higher-level instructor, and how to keep training safe and consistent.

Step 2

Use flashcards

Run cards in shuffled mode. Mark cards you miss and repeat only the weak deck until you can explain the answer without seeing it.

Step 3

Practice the practical

Rehearse preparation, classroom setup, lesson delivery, adjustment, testing, feedback, and documentation out loud—not just in your head.

Step 4

Take the full exam

Use the 90-minute timer. Your goal is not merely 80%; aim for 90% or better twice before testing.

14-day plan

  1. Days 1–2: Professional role + learning principles
  2. Days 3–4: Planning + materials
  3. Days 5–6: Learning environment + delivery
  4. Days 7–8: Skills training + testing
  5. Day 9: Records + full review
  6. Days 10–11: Flashcards and weak areas
  7. Day 12: Practical rehearsal
  8. Day 13: Timed 100-question test
  9. Day 14: Review misses, then stop cramming early

3-day cram plan

  1. Day 1: Modules 1–5 + 40 flashcards
  2. Day 2: Modules 6–10 + practical checklist
  3. Day 3: Full mock exam → review only missed topics → one short flashcard pass

If your mock score is below 80%, do not just retake the same questions. Re-study the topic attached to each miss.

Hands-on preparation

Practical / JPR readiness

The exact stations depend on the adopted standard and the JPRs selected for your exam. Train the process until you can perform it without prompts.

01

Prepare the assignment

  • Read the prepared lesson plan completely.
  • Identify objectives, prerequisites, safety requirements, equipment, student materials, evaluation method, and time.
  • Verify course materials are complete and current.
  • Know what you may adapt versus what you may not fundamentally change.
02

Set the learning environment

  • Check lighting, temperature/weather, noise, seating, exits, access, and distractions.
  • Inspect training props and audiovisual equipment.
  • Identify hazards before students enter the evolution.
  • Have a backup plan for equipment or weather problems.
03

Open the lesson

  • Introduce yourself and establish relevance.
  • State what the students will be able to do.
  • Connect the lesson to prior knowledge or job performance.
  • State safety expectations before skill activity begins.
04

Present and demonstrate

  • Follow the prepared plan and required method.
  • Communicate clearly and use aids at the correct time.
  • For skills: demonstrate correctly, break the task into teachable steps, then supervise practice.
  • Check understanding with questions and observation.
05

Adjust without losing the objective

  • Adapt pace, examples, seating, media, grouping, or instructional method when needed.
  • Address disruptive behavior professionally.
  • Provide remediation when a learner struggles.
  • Never trade safety or the stated learning outcome for speed.
06

Evaluate and document

  • Administer the assigned written, oral, or performance evaluation consistently.
  • Protect test security and avoid coaching during evaluation.
  • Provide appropriate feedback after the evaluation.
  • Record attendance, completion, results, and other required documentation accurately.

Rehearsal drill

Run a 15-minute mini-class

Pick a simple firefighter skill you already know well—such as tying a clove hitch, selecting a portable extinguisher, or performing an SCBA inspection. Use an existing lesson outline. Have another firefighter intentionally ask a confusing question, create one minor equipment problem, and make one learner need extra help. Your job is to maintain the objective, safety, continuity, and student engagement.

Evaluator mindset

During a certification practical, narrate the important decisions that may not be obvious to the evaluator: “I verified the lesson objectives,” “I checked the prop before class,” “I am stopping this attempt because it is unsafe,” or “I am recording the result according to the AHJ procedure.” Do not assume the evaluator can infer a required step you never demonstrated.

Standards & source note

Built to stay useful through the NFPA transition

NFPA 1041 historically contained Fire and Emergency Services Instructor professional qualifications. NFPA's current consolidated professional-qualifications standard is NFPA 1020 (2025), which now includes Instructor, Fire Officer, and EMS Officer qualifications. IFSTA's 9th-edition Instructor manual covers NFPA 1041 (2019); IFSTA's 10th edition aligns to NFPA 1020 (2025).

Colorado DFPC documents currently accessible online show Fire & Emergency Services Instructor I testing as 100 questions, 80% required, 1½ hours, plus mandatory/random practical JPRs requiring 100% completion. Because those Colorado documents are older, verify the exact standard edition and test process with DFPC or your department training officer before the test.
