Before you train: CPAT preparation is strenuous. Increase workload progressively, practice safe lifting/carrying mechanics, stop for concerning symptoms, and follow medical advice or restrictions that apply to you. Do not improvise unsafe ladders, weighted vests, tunnels, striking devices, or overhead props.

Four workout types

Choose the session that fits today's goal. Quality and consistency beat turning every workout into a maximal test.

No CPAT equipment? Train the movement pattern.

Stairs

Stair machine, stadium steps, step-ups, uphill walking.

Hose / rescue drag

Sled drag, tire drag, heavy rope pull, backward loaded drag.

Equipment carry

Farmer carries with dumbbells or kettlebells.

Ladder / ceiling

Controlled rows, pulldowns, overhead press/pull patterns with safe gym equipment.

Forcible-entry demand

Rotational power and controlled striking only where safe, permitted, and properly supervised.

Search

Bear crawls, low crawls, direction changes, and getting up from the floor under fatigue.

Test-day readiness checklist

Arrive rested, organized, and familiar with your provider's rules.

Final 72 hours

  1. Do not cram fitness. You cannot build meaningful conditioning in the final two days.
  2. Keep moving lightly. Easy mobility or low-intensity activity is usually more useful than another exhausting simulation.
  3. Use normal food and hydration habits. Test day is not the time for unfamiliar supplements or drastic diet changes.
  4. Verify logistics. Test site, arrival time, identification, clothing, footwear, and provider instructions.
  5. Know your pacing plan. The goal is to finish the entire course—not win the first event.
