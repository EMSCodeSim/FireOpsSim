Your HazMat prep plan

Build recognition and the Operations role first. Then drill ERG use and defensive decision-making under time pressure.

Step 1

Master the boundaries

Know what Awareness, Operations, and Technician responders may do. This prevents both test errors and unsafe field decisions.

Step 2

Drill the ERG

Practice ID number → yellow, name → blue, guide → orange, and green-table protective-action decisions until lookup becomes automatic.

Step 3

Use flashcards

Mark misses and repeat the weak deck. Placards, properties, PPE, zones, decon, and role limits should become quick recall.

Step 4

Take the full exam

The simulator grades Awareness and Operations separately. Your goal is 90%+ overall and no section below 80%.

Quick skill drill

ERG section trainer

Build the lookup pathway before the test and before you need it on an incident.

1

Known ID number

Yellow pages. Find the UN/NA four-digit number, then follow it to the assigned orange guide.

2

Known material name

Blue pages. Find the proper shipping name alphabetically, then follow it to the orange guide.

3

Orange guide

Review potential hazards, public safety, fire/spill response, first aid, evacuation, and protective-clothing guidance for the guide.

4

Green-table material

Use the green tables and notes for initial isolation/protective action when the material is specifically listed. Account for spill size, day/night, and wind direction.

5-minute station drill

Use a real 2024 ERG

Have a partner call out five UN/NA numbers and five material names. Find the guide number, identify the primary hazard, and state whether green-table information applies. Do not memorize isolated guide numbers—the skill is fast, accurate lookup.

FireOpsSim tools

Use the existing HazMat Placard Reference, Isolation Planner, Decon Corridor Planner, and HazMat Size-Up Checklist as supplemental drills. Always verify operational decisions against the current ERG, SOPs, and command.

Hands-on preparation

Operations practical / JPR readiness

Exact stations depend on the adopted standard and selected JPRs. Train the process and your department equipment until you can perform without prompts.

01

Analyze the incident

  • Recognize a HazMat/WMD incident and identify hazards from a safe position.
  • Use container/marking/shipping-document clues and the ERG.
  • State responder-level limitations and request needed resources.
  • Identify protective actions and communication needs.
02

Establish control

  • Initiate/operate within ICS.
  • Isolate and deny entry.
  • Set appropriate hot/warm/cold zones and control access.
  • Maintain accountability, escape routes, and changing-condition awareness.
03

Select PPE

  • Identify the hazard and exposure route.
  • Select only PPE authorized and appropriate for the assignment.
  • Demonstrate inspection/donning/use limitations as required.
  • Recognize heat stress, air-management, and contamination concerns.
04

Defensive control

  • Choose diking, damming, diversion, absorption/adsorption, runoff control, or other allowed defensive tactics.
  • State compatibility and environmental concerns.
  • Maintain a safe position and do not cross into Technician source control.
  • Reassess tactic effectiveness and changing conditions.
05

Decontamination

  • Establish dirty-to-clean flow and control access.
  • Select emergency/gross/basic decon based on the hazard and objective.
  • Use PPE for decon personnel.
  • Prevent runoff/secondary contamination and coordinate patient transfer.
06

Terminate safely

  • Account for personnel and exposures.
  • Manage contaminated PPE/tools/equipment.
  • Complete required documentation and medical follow-up.
  • Restore readiness and participate in the post-incident review.

Scenario drill

Run a 20-minute tabletop

Use a tanker, railcar, industrial release, or unknown-drum scenario. Require the student to approach/position, identify clues, use the ERG, establish zones, choose PPE, select one defensive control action, set decon, and explain termination. Inject a wind shift halfway through.

Evaluator mindset

During a certification practical, verbalize required decisions that may not be visible: “I am approaching upwind,” “this is beyond Operations source-control capability,” “I am protecting the storm drain defensively,” or “this tool remains contaminated and will not return to service.” Follow the actual Colorado skill sheet and your evaluator’s directions.

Standards & source note

Know which standard your test uses

Colorado DFPC documents publicly accessible online show a 100-question Hazardous Materials Awareness/Operations exam composed of 25 Awareness and 75 Operations questions, with 80% required in each section and a 90-minute time limit, plus mandatory/random Operations practical JPRs requiring full completion. Those documents reference older NFPA 472-era material, so candidates should verify the exact current edition on their test authorization.

NFPA 470 is the consolidated Hazardous Materials/Weapons of Mass Destruction responder standard and combines material formerly found in NFPA 472, NFPA 1072, and NFPA 473. OSHA 29 CFR 1910.120(q)(6)(ii) continues to define Operations responders as defensive responders and identifies the core Operations competencies. PHMSA’s current public Emergency Response Guidebook is the 2024 ERG.
