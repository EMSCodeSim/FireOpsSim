Career-long readiness

Protect the firefighter, not only the assignment.

Operational readiness includes exposure reduction, medical follow-up, fitness, sleep, behavioral health, relationships, and asking for help early. Use this page as an action organizer, then follow department programs and qualified medical guidance.

Practical actions

Build repeatable protective habits.

Know the domains

Health and safety are connected.

Exposure

Cancer risk reduction

Reduce avoidable contamination, document significant exposures, follow gear and station-cleaning procedures, and use occupational-health guidance.

Cardiovascular

Medical readiness

Know personal risk factors, follow clinician guidance, build fitness progressively, and do not ignore concerning exertional symptoms.

Behavioral

Mental health and substance use

Watch for persistent changes in sleep, mood, anger, concentration, avoidance, alcohol or drug use, and relationships.

Fatigue

Sleep and driving safety

Emergency work disrupts sleep. Treat dangerous drowsiness as an immediate safety problem, especially before driving.

Recovery

Injury and return to duty

Use qualified evaluation, progressive rehabilitation, job-specific testing, and department return-to-work procedures.

Family

Relationships and identity

Create transition rituals, communicate schedule impacts, maintain interests outside the job, and include family in support planning.
