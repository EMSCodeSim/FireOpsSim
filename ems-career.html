Stage 01

EMT school

Build a repeatable assessment process and understand the credentialing path before graduation.

Know the sequence

1Complete an eligible EMT education program and the program requirements.
2Complete the National Registry application and examination process required for national certification.
3Complete the state EMS office’s licensure or certification process for the state where you intend to practice.
4Complete employer credentialing, orientation, FTO requirements, driving requirements, and local protocol education.
Important distinction: National Registry certification is not a license to practice. Legal authority to practice comes from the state or other legally recognized licensing authority.

What to be good at before your first job

Patient introductions and consent, primary assessment, focused history, accurate vitals, safe lifting/movement, reassessment, concise handoff, documentation basics, equipment familiarity, PPE, roadway awareness, and knowing when to ask for help.

Do not study only for the exam

Use scenarios that make you discover information through questions and assessment. The first-job problem is rarely “Can I recognize a vocabulary word?” It is “Can I gather enough good information to make the next safe decision?”

Stage 02

New EMT: your first field year

The goal is reliable basics, good communication, and steady improvement—not trying to look experienced.

The first-year priorities

01System knowledge. Learn response areas, hospitals, radio procedures, unit setup, supply replacement, and local operational routines.
02Assessment consistency. Run the same strong foundational process on ordinary calls so it is available under stress.
03Communication. Get comfortable talking with patients, partners, nurses, family, dispatch, and other responders.
04Documentation. Learn what your service expects, how to describe findings objectively, and how to document reassessment.
05Feedback. Ask after routine calls: one thing to keep, one thing to change.
06Professional record. Track skills, training and milestones without patient identifiers.

Stage 03

Paramedic school

Advanced education adds complexity. Your organization system matters almost as much as your study system.

Keep clinical requirements visible

Track classroom requirements, laboratory competencies, clinical experiences, field internship requirements, deadlines and preceptor feedback in one place. Do not wait until the final weeks to discover a missing requirement.

Train decisions, not only recall

Build a habit of identifying the immediate threat, developing a differential, choosing priorities, checking medications deliberately, communicating a plan and reassessing whether it worked.

Medication safety is a behavior

Use a consistent verification process for medication, concentration, dose, route, patient, indication, contraindications and local protocol requirements. Follow your program, agency and medical direction.

Practice the whole encounter

A strong scenario includes scene leadership, history, exam, treatment priorities, delegation, destination/transport reasoning, consultation when required, handoff and documentation—not just a diagnosis.

Paramedic program accreditation, clinical experiences, field internship, certification eligibility and state licensing requirements are program- and jurisdiction-specific. Use your program director, National Registry candidate materials and state EMS office as the source of truth.

Stage 04

New paramedic

Your first year as the highest-level clinician on a scene is about reducing variability and becoming safely independent.

Build a deliberate first-year routine

1Set one clinical theme for a block of shifts: respiratory, shock, neurologic, pediatrics, medication safety, handoff, scene leadership or another local need.
2Review a relevant local protocol after calls and compare what you actually did with your system’s expectations.
3Ask for feedback on decision quality, pacing, delegation and communication—not only whether the final treatment was correct.
4Use QA/QI and case review when available to learn from outcomes without turning every call into a private patient database.
5Keep a de-identified career log of skills, training, leadership and important learning points for future FTO, specialty or promotional applications.

Stage 05

Recertification

Start early enough that continuing education supports your practice instead of becoming a deadline scramble.

Current National Registry NCCP totals

Under the 2025 NCCP model currently in use, the National Registry lists the following total continuing-education requirements for recertification. State licensure renewal can have additional or different requirements.

16EMR total credits
40EMT total credits
50AEMT total credits
60Paramedic total credits

National Registry credentials are generally maintained on a two-year recertification cycle. Check the expiration date in your account and your state’s separate renewal rules.

Make CE useful

Use the local/state component for protocol and system needs; use flexible education to target your actual clinical gaps. Keep certificates and course records organized as you go.

Track two clocks

Your National Registry certification and your state authorization to practice are related but distinct. Put both expiration dates in your certification tracker instead of assuming one renewal automatically handles the other.

Stage 06

Returning after time away

Do not guess at a lapsed-credential pathway. Current status changes what route applies.

Start by separating three questions

1Is your National Registry credential current, inactive or lapsed?
2Is your state EMS license/certification current or lapsed?
3What does the state where you want to work require now?

The National Registry publishes separate full-education, state-licensed and re-entry pathways. The right route depends on your current status and level.

Protocols, equipment, terminology, scope and evidence can change materially during a career gap. Credential restoration is only one part of safe re-entry; plan a structured clinical and operational refresh with the agency that will credential you.
