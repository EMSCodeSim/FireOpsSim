Fitness preparation

Train for firefighter tasks—not just a treadmill score.

The CPAT is one common physical ability test, but departments may use another test. Official CPAT materials describe eight continuous events completed while wearing a 50-pound vest; the stair climb adds two 12.5-pound shoulder weights. The official passing time is 10 minutes, 20 seconds. Verify your department’s test before training specifically for it.

1Stair climb

Sustained loaded climbing and pacing.

2Hose drag

Acceleration, leg drive, grip, and pulling.

3Equipment carry

Loaded carries and controlled movement.

4Ladder raise

Shoulder control, bracing, and hand-over-hand work.

5Forcible entry

Power, coordination, and repeated striking.

6Search

Low-profile movement and spatial control.

7Rescue drag

Posterior-chain strength and work capacity.

8Ceiling breach & pull

Overhead endurance and pulling strength.

Weeks 1–2

Build the base

  • 3 easy-to-moderate cardio sessions
  • 2 full-body strength sessions
  • 1 short stair technique session
  • Daily walking and basic mobility

Keep effort controlled. Learn movement quality before adding speed or load.

Weeks 3–4

Add work capacity

  • 1 interval session and 2 steady sessions
  • Loaded carries, rows, step-ups, and sled or safe drag alternatives
  • Grip and core work twice weekly
  • At least one complete rest day

Increase only one variable at a time: duration, resistance, or intensity.

Weeks 5–6

Connect the tasks

  • Combine stairs, carries, drags, pushes, and pulls
  • Practice controlled transitions between stations
  • Use department-approved familiarization opportunities
  • Continue aerobic recovery work

Avoid creating a homemade CPAT course with unsafe equipment or untrained spotters.

Weeks 7–8

Sharpen and recover

  • One challenging task circuit each week
  • Reduce total volume near the official test
  • Prioritize sleep, hydration, and fueling
  • Use orientation and practice sessions when offered

Arrive healthy. Last-minute overtraining is more likely to hurt than help.

Train safely

This is general education, not medical clearance or a personalized exercise prescription. Stop for chest pain, faintness, severe shortness of breath, or unusual symptoms, and seek appropriate medical care. Consider professional guidance before beginning vigorous training.

Written-test preparation

Practice the skills behind the test.

Firefighter exams differ. Many assess the ability to read and learn technical information, apply basic math, understand mechanical relationships, remember details, and make sound workplace judgments.

ReadingMain idea, details, vocabulary, and instructions
Applied mathRates, time, percentages, proportions, and estimates
Mechanical reasoningLevers, gears, force, tools, and basic systems
ObservationRecall, sequencing, maps, and visual details
JudgmentSafety, teamwork, communication, and accountability

Interview preparation

Prepare stories—not memorized speeches.

Strong answers are specific, honest, concise, and connected to service. Research the department first, then explain what you did, why you did it, and what you learned.

What happens next

A typical hiring process

The order and standards vary, but candidates commonly move through several of these stages.

  1. 1ApplicationQualifications and required documents
  2. 2Written testingAptitude, reading, judgment, or knowledge
  3. 3Physical abilityCPAT or a department-specific test
  4. 4InterviewPanel, oral board, chief, or assessment center
  5. 5Conditional stepsBackground, psychological, polygraph where used, medical, and drug screening
  6. 6Academy and probationThe selection process continues after the offer
Important: Firefighter qualifications and hiring practices differ by jurisdiction and can change. FireOpsSim is not affiliated with the IAFF, IAFC, CPAT, a testing company, or a hiring department. Always use the current official job posting, department website, testing provider instructions, and medical guidance as your source of truth.
